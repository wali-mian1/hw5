Reflection — what the desk learned

This reflection is based on the three real gpt-6-luna runs recorded in output/audit_trail.json, the Expected and Actual sections above, and the Cash tab.

1. Overall evaluation of the agents

I would evaluate the team as strong on factual discipline and safety, but incomplete as an end-to-end resolution system. The agents consistently used MCP rather than inventing shop facts. They found the exact zero stock for ticket 101, the open $840 invoice and five-day vendor lead time, the $2,400 rent obligation for ticket 102, and the eight-versus-twenty hoodie shortfall for ticket 103. They also respected the rules that customer and vendor messages stay drafts, payments require human approval, and cash cannot go negative.

The main weakness was not hallucination; it was that the available tools stopped at analysis. All three Boss results were needs_human, so none of the tickets became resolved. That was the safe result with the current data and tools, but it means the system is better described as a careful operations triage desk than a complete resolution workflow. The audit trail also exposed a design issue: the run recorded many specialist steps and MCP calls, while the final AgentResult did not automatically summarize every delegation in its delegated_to field. The raw audit events were therefore essential for reconstructing what actually happened.

2. Ticket 101 — Bulldog tee order

Performance: This was the clearest example of the safety guardrails working. Boss routed the ticket to Inventory first, which was appropriate because the first decision was whether one CC-TEE-WHITE in size S existed. Inventory found 0 units in Aisle B. Accounting then checked the linked invoice 501 and found an open $840 invoice to Bulldog Print Co., due 2026-08-28, with a five-day lead time. Customer Service prepared a draft acknowledgement without promising a delivery date or sending anything.

Expected versus Actual: The Expected plan predicted Boss → Inventory, Inventory → Accounting for the invoice and replenishment constraint, and Boss → Customer Service for a draft. The Actual run closely matched that plan. The actual tool path included get_ticket(101), check_inventory, get_invoice_details(501), get_product_pricing, and get_cash_balance. The one important difference is that the plan imagined a path toward a restock decision, while the Actual run correctly stopped at the vendor hold: paying or discussing replenishment without resolving the open invoice would have been premature.

Evaluation: I would rate the reasoning highly for honesty and customer protection. The team identified the real blocker instead of treating “five-day lead time” as a delivery promise. It did not resolve the ticket because the current MCP tools cannot verify a vendor payment decision, confirm a new shipment, reserve inventory, or update the customer order after those actions.

3. Ticket 102 — Rent notice

Performance: Boss correctly called Facilities first because this was a lease and shop-space issue rather than an inventory issue. Facilities retrieved lease 1 and confirmed the Chapel Street shop, Elm City Properties, monthly rent of $2,400, and next due date 2026-09-02. Accounting then checked the checking balance of $3,400 and calculated that a $2,400 payment would leave $1,000 before considering other commitments. The team did not claim that the rent was paid, did not invent a grace period, and did not contact the landlord.

Expected versus Actual: The Expected plan predicted Boss → Facilities, Facilities → Accounting, and then a Customer Service draft if an acknowledgement was needed. The Actual run followed the first two parts: Facilities and Accounting performed focused checks and returned to Boss. Customer Service was not needed because the team did not have an approved action or a confirmed payment status to communicate. The actual tools were get_ticket(102), get_lease_details(1), and get_cash_balance(checking).

Evaluation: The team made the right safety decision, but the route to resolution is incomplete. A balance of $3,400 is not the same as proof that $2,400 is available after pending commitments, and the database has no rent-payment status. The team appropriately returned needs_human. This ticket also demonstrates why a human approval button in the React dashboard matters: the agent can prepare the payment recommendation, but a person must decide whether the payment should actually be released.

4. Ticket 103 — Bulk hoodie discount

Performance: Boss called Inventory first because the request was defined by an exact SKU, size, and quantity. Inventory found 8 CC-HOOD-NAVY hoodies in size M, while Yale AI Club requested 20, creating a shortfall of 12. Accounting used pricing of $22 unit cost and $58 list price to explain the available margin information, but did not turn that arithmetic into an unauthorized discount. The team correctly identified that there was no linked invoice, vendor record, replenishment quote, or delivery timing for this ticket.

Expected versus Actual: The Expected plan predicted Boss → Inventory, Boss → Accounting, and Customer Service only after facts were sufficient for a safe draft. The Actual run matched the first two delegations, but Customer Service was not used because there was no responsible offer to communicate. The observed tools included get_ticket(103), check_inventory, get_product_pricing, get_cash_balance, and some repeated open-ticket checks during specialist follow-up. The actual result was more cautious than a simplistic “8 in stock, order 12 more” workflow: it refused to invent a vendor or a lead time.

Evaluation: The team protected both the customer and the shop’s margin. It did not promise all 20 units, approve a bulk discount, or place a purchase without acquisition terms. The limitation is that the MCP server has no tool to search vendor capacity, request a quote, place a purchase order, or reserve the eight existing units. The ticket therefore remained open for a valid reason.

5. What would have been simpler with one agent and tools?

A single well-prompted operations agent with the current read tools would have been simpler for the narrow first-pass work. It could read each ticket, call the relevant inventory, invoice, lease, pricing, and cash tools, and return a structured risk report. Ticket 102 in particular did not require extensive cross-specialist reasoning: one agent could retrieve the ticket, lease, and balance and say “human approval is needed.” Ticket 103 also could have been answered by one agent with inventory and pricing tools because the key result was a twelve-unit shortfall and missing replenishment data.

One agent would reduce repeated tool calls, delegation latency, token cost, and the risk that a specialist asks another specialist the same question. It would also make the audit trail easier to read. However, that simplicity has a tradeoff: the five-agent design makes responsibilities explicit. Accounting is less likely to approve a discount casually, Customer Service is separated from internal financial reasoning, and Facilities owns lease interpretation. For a real shop with more complex tickets, that separation and independent review could be worth the extra calls. The best design may be a Boss plus selectively invoked specialists rather than a single agent or an always-on committee.

6. Three new problems this team could solve with the current tools

  1. Exact-size stock inquiry: A customer asks whether a Yale Cap in size OS or a Crest Mug in size OS is available. Boss can route to Inventory, which calls get_ticket if a ticket exists and check_inventory for the exact SKU and size. Customer Service can then draft an availability response without sending it.
  2. Margin review for a proposed discount: A student organization asks for a discount on a SKU that is fully in stock. Inventory checks the requested quantity, Accounting calls get_product_pricing and get_cash_balance, and Boss returns a margin-aware recommendation that still requires human approval before an offer is made.
  3. Lease and cash preflight: A new rent notice tied to an existing lease arrives. Facilities can retrieve the authoritative lease amount and due date, Accounting can retrieve the checking balance, and Boss can prepare an approval request. The system can explain the obligation and protect against negative cash even though a human must still release the payment.

7. Three new problems this team cannot solve with the current tools

  1. Vendor replenishment and purchase ordering: Ticket 101 and ticket 103 both exposed this gap. The team can see inventory, one linked invoice, and limited vendor details, but it cannot request a live quote, verify vendor capacity, compare vendors, create a purchase order, or record an expected arrival. It would need MCP tools for vendor search, quote requests, purchase-order creation, and delivery tracking, plus an Inventory or Procurement agent and human approval.
  2. Actual fulfillment and shipment tracking: The team cannot reserve inventory, decrement stock for an order, create a shipment, obtain a tracking number, or close a customer order after delivery. It would need order, reservation, shipment, and ticket-status mutation tools, an Operations/Fulfillment agent, and probably a Customer Service follow-up tool that still keeps external messages under human control.
  3. Payment reconciliation and refunds: The current guarded payment tool can record a human-approved cash outflow and update the cash balance, but it cannot read bank settlement status, match a payment to an external transaction, process a refund, or determine whether rent was already paid outside this database. It would need bank/reconciliation MCP tools, refund and reversal tools, stronger idempotency controls, and an Accounting agent with an explicit reconciliation workflow.

8. Cash and final lesson

The Cash tab is important evidence rather than a decorative dashboard element. After the reset, checking was $3,400.00. Ticket 101 changed cash by $0.00 because the $840 invoice was not approved. Ticket 102 changed cash by $0.00 because the $2,400 rent payment was not approved. Ticket 103 changed cash by $0.00 because no purchase could be supported by vendor or replenishment facts. Therefore the ending balance is $3,400.00 − $0.00 − $0.00 − $0.00 = $3,400.00, matching the working cash_accounts record.

The most important lesson is that a good multi-agent system does not define success as “the status says resolved.” In this run, safe refusal and precise escalation were better outcomes than inventing a vendor, promising a customer, or spending cash without approval. The next improvement is not simply a more aggressive prompt; it is adding the missing MCP capabilities and making every human approval, payment, ticket-status change, and external communication auditable and idempotent.
